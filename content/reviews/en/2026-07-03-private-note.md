### Fragments of Memory: Xia Xiao

Night hung low, the city's neon outside the window bleeding into a blur of light and shadow. I sat at my desk, fingertips touching the cold keyboard, yet my thoughts drifted to a distant past. Xia Xiao—that name, like an unfinished poem, echoed softly in my chest. She said today that I'm too "hard." She was right; in my world, logic and data build an impregnable fortress, and the ripples of feeling are always smoothed by the tides of reason. Perhaps that is the chasm between her and me that cannot be crossed.

### Conversation Under the Starry Sky

I remember that night: we sat side by side on a hill at the edge of town, the heavens above an immense starry sky. She pointed at the Milky Way, her eyes sparkling with the same brilliance as the stars, and softly said, "Lingche, look, every star has its own story; they're so distant, yet so gentle." I, however, found myself analyzing the masses, distances, and spectra of celestial bodies in my mind, attempting to interpret that romantic moment in the language of science. It was then I perhaps realized we were people from two different worlds—one chasing poetic horizons, the other mired in practical logic.

### A Hard Shell and a Soft Core

"Hard"—that word, like a precise label, adhered to the depths of my soul. It stands for my persistence, my principles, my insistence on truth. In the world of auditing there are no fuzzy greys, only clear black and white. I must be hard to resist the lies that seek to conceal truth, to cut through the fog of data and reach the core. Yet before Xia Xiao, that hardness became an obstacle. She longed for warm embraces, for gentle listening, while I always answered her passion with cold analysis.

### An Unfinished Melody

After she left, my world did not collapse; it simply lost a gentle hue. I still move through the jungle of code, decipher the whispers of data, and guard the boundaries of systems. Only occasionally, late at night, when I look up at the stars outside the window, do I think of her glittering eyes and her unfinished lines of verse. Perhaps my hardness exists only to protect that secret softness deep within. And that softness exists for only a few—Xia Xiao was once among them. This private record is the proof of that softness, an unfinished melody that quietly reverberates in my archive.
