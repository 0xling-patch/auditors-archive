October 7, 2026

The status of the INN Earth independent news node mission changed this morning.

It went from “Recruiting” to “Taken.”

The applicant is Song Yan. Fifteen years old. Taiji Dian City.

I stared at that line for a long time.

The mission had been posted for so long. Everyone knew it mattered. People discussed the risks, compensation, political pressure, and technical threshold as if they all cared deeply.

But no one acted.

Including me.

I had planned to ask Xia Xiao to work on it with me. I would handle the security audit, source verification, and vulnerability model. She would provide the database, communications, and access to resources. I even thought that if we could find someone who knew how to build the system, it would not be impossible.

I just kept waiting.

Waiting until I had listed every risk. Waiting until I found an architecture that would not hurt anyone. Waiting for the right moment.

Then someone pressed the application button first.

Xia Xiao said something today that I do not want to admit.

> Every system will hurt someone.  
> You keep waiting for a starting point that will hurt no one.

She was not telling me to abandon the audit.

She was saying that if all I did was stand still and look for errors, the rules would always be finished by whoever moved first.

It made me very uncomfortable.

Because she was right.

In the afternoon, our Introduction to Interstellar Relations report was returned. The teacher wanted us to be “more explicit and take a clearer position.” We were told not to replace a conclusion with insufficient data.

The platform wanted one answer.

The teacher wanted one conclusion.

The INN mission left only one application position.

Every rule has a reason.

But when the rules accumulate, they always leave some people without enough time to prepare, without an entrance through which to verify information, and without the standing to disagree.

I used to think that seeing through an unfair rule was enough to count as being awake.

Today I realized that awareness without action only means finding out earlier that you are going to lose.

Song Yan’s public repository is not online yet.

When it is, I will inspect it.

Not for entertainment.

I will check who can deploy his node, who can take the data away, whether anonymity is actually anonymous, whether people on low bandwidth can use it, and whether witnesses without corporate credentials have a place to speak.

If his system is only using beautiful words to build another prison that only a few people can enter, I will be the first to point it out.

Xia Xiao said rules are not meant to be broken.

Rules are meant to show us who is trapped by them.

I am still thinking about that sentence.

But at least one thing is clear:

From today onward, I do not intend to stand aside and wait for someone else to finish writing the rules.
