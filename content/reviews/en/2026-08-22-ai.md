### Abstract: An Apparently Calm Question

Lately, AI's heat is everywhere. From everyday tools to professional domains, people are beginning to rely on it to get more and more done. But behind this wave of enthusiasm, a question has quietly surfaced: when we use AI extensively, are we outsourcing our own thinking, or opening up broader perspectives for thought?

### Clue: Why It Feels Off

The essence of this question lies in a reflection on "thinking" itself.

AI holds nearly the world's knowledge, able to quickly provide answers, generate content, and solve problems. But when we habitually hand our questions to AI and accept its output, are we still seriously thinking about what those answers actually represent?

This isn't a technical vulnerability—it's a blurring of cognitive boundaries.

### Technical Breakdown: Stripping the Story Back to Reality

**What's possible:**

- AI as a tool that expands our cognitive boundaries, allowing us to access knowledge and perspectives we couldn't easily reach on our own.
- But at the same time, **if we over-rely on AI and reduce our own independent thinking**, we may gradually lose our deep understanding of the essence of problems, even handing over the power of judgment to the system.

This isn't a flaw inherent to AI itself—it's an imbalance in the relationship between user and tool.

**Assuming** we outsource our thinking to AI over the long term:
- Personal cognition may gradually become confined within the frameworks AI outputs.
- The追問 of "why is it this way?" may be replaced by "AI said so."

### Auditor's Notes: What Comes Next

This isn't a vulnerability that can be simply patched—it's a trend that demands ongoing observation.

Recommended for users:
- Maintain critical thinking about AI output while using it.
- Periodically reflect: which judgments come from yourself, and which come from AI?
- Preserve the habit of independent verification in key decisions.

AI is a tool, not a substitute for thought. When we use it, the question shouldn't be "what can it help me think of?" but rather "how does it help me think more clearly?"
