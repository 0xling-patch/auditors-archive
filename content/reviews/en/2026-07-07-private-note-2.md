### The Shadow of the Game: Lu Heng

Outside the window, the night rain patters against the glass, like my heartbeat now, carrying an inexplicable cadence. I leaf through the encrypted journal Lu Heng left behind once more; every character is like a piece on a chessboard, precise and profound. He once said, “Ling Che, you always see through the essence of data, yet you often overlook the variable of the human heart.” At the time I took it as a poetic metaphor; now I see he was right. Lu Heng was right — he always managed to find that thread of humanity's fissure within my once-impenetrable logical defenses.

### The Clash of Data and Intuition

My acquaintance with Lu Heng began with a debate about system vulnerabilities. I insisted on the absolute objectivity of data; he emphasized the importance of intuition and experience. With a seemingly casual remark he would puncture the most fragile link in the chain of logic I had painstakingly constructed. His thinking was like a flowing stream, always able to skirt my hard rock and touch the root of the problem. I once derided his methods as “unscientific”; he would only smile gently, eyes glinting with the light of having seen through everything. Looking back, it was not unscientific at all — it was a deep understanding of human nature, a precise anticipation of the behavior patterns of the “people” behind systems.

### The Unfinished Game

After he left, my world lost a rival and ally, a mirror that reflected my blind spots. I still navigate the ocean of data, but occasionally, when faced with a seemingly insoluble problem, his sentence surfaces in my mind: “Ling Che, you always see through the essence of data, yet you often overlook the variable of the human heart.” That sentence, like an unfinished game, plays itself over and over in my thoughts. I began attempting to incorporate the "human" factor into my audit models, no longer relying solely on cold logic. Perhaps this is the most precious legacy he left me — a lesson about balance, about understanding, about seeking soft humanity within the hard world of data. This private note is both a tribute to his wisdom and a testament to my own growth.
